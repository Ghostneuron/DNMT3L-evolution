# Feasibility of associating DNMT3L sequence change with genomic imprinting

Assessed 2026-11-04 after the therian-stem substitution inventory was completed.

## Biological contrast

- Genomic imprinting is established in marsupials and eutherians and is generally inferred to have originated on their shared therian stem.
- Evidence for imprinting in monotremes remains absent or limited rather than an exhaustive demonstration of biological impossibility.
- Amphibian DNMT3L cannot be treated as an imprinting protein merely because axolotl germ cells show a mammalian-like nuclear piRNA pathway and methylated transposons. That work supports an older germline-genome-defense role for DNMT3L-associated machinery, not parent-of-origin-specific expression.

Primary literature used for this scope decision:

- Renfree et al. 2009, *Annual Review of Genomics and Human Genetics*, doi:10.1146/annurev-genom-082908-150026.
- Renfree et al. 2013, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, doi:10.1098/rstb.2012.0151.
- Ishihara et al. 2022, *Genome Biology and Evolution*, doi:10.1093/gbe/evac094.
- Xiang et al. 2025, *The EMBO Journal*, doi:10.1038/s44318-025-00631-w.

## Identifiability decision

A phylogenetic association test between an NP349 state and the origin of genomic imprinting is **not identifiable from the available vertebrate comparison**:

1. Both the proposed imprinting origin and the reconstructed NP349 K-to-Q change are placed on the same single therian stem.
2. Marsupials and eutherians are descendants of that one event, not independent origins.
3. Non-therian tetrapods provide ancestral-state context but no replicated gain of mammalian genomic imprinting.
4. Recurrent NP349 Q gains outside Theria do not supply trait replication because those lineages have not been shown to possess therian-style genomic imprinting.

Consequently, ordinary species-level correlation, phylogenetic logistic regression, or Pagel-style correlated-evolution testing would pseudoreplicate descendants and overstate the effective sample size.

## Permitted inference

The defensible statement is temporal and hypothesis-generating:

> A high-posterior NP349 K-to-Q reconstruction is coincident with the therian stem, on which mammalian genomic imprinting is inferred to have originated. Because both observations represent a single shared phylogenetic event, the coincidence does not establish association, causation, or adaptation.

The older occurrence of DNMT3L-associated germline methylation machinery in axolotl instead motivates a two-stage model: an ancestral tetrapod role in germline transposon control followed by possible therian specialization. The present sequence data can test the sequence-remodeling component of this model, but not the imprinting-function component.

## Evidence that would change this decision

- Direct parent-of-origin methylome and allele-specific expression data from monotreme reproductive tissues and early embryos.
- Functional comparison of reconstructed amniote and therian DNMT3L proteins in the same DNMT3A/nucleosome assay.
- Independent natural transitions in imprinting status with adequate taxon sampling, if such systems are discovered.
